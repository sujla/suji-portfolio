Pickup Order UX
Enhancement

E-commerce•Data-driven Design•Conversion Optimization

Project

Dec 2024 – Jan 2025

Role

Product Design Lead

Team

  • 1 Product Manager
  • 2 Software Engineers

Platform

Mobile Web

Contributions

    Context

    Users checking store availability were already close to purchase.

    51.5% of users on the available-stores page took active shopping actions such as searching, filtering, or selecting store cards. Yet the page did not make the pickup path immediately clear.

    Behavioral signals

    User behavior showed a gap between
    intent and action.

    1. Strong engagement, weak add-to-cart

    Active shopping behavior was high, but add-to-cart remained low.

    2. High store interest, but rapid return

    Despite strong store-detail views, over 80% of users returned to the previous page within 1–2 minutes.

    Problem

    Users wanted pickup, but the experience
    did not lead them there.

    The behavioral signals suggested that users were ready to move forward, but the experience was not effectively turning that intent into pickup orders.

    UX diagnosis

    The interface did not clearly guide
    users toward pickup.

    1. Pickup CTA lacked visibility

    The pickup action competed with other information and was easy to miss.

    2. Store cards suggested the wrong action

    Users frequently selected store cards expecting the next step toward purchase, but the interaction led them elsewhere.

    3. Actions did not reflect store availability

    Different stock and pickup conditions were not translated into clear, contextual actions for users.

    Goals

    Make pickup easier to find, understand,
    and complete.

    User Goal

    Make the pickup order button immediately visible.

    Service Goal

    Reflect each store’s availability and conditions clearly on the store card.

    Business Goal

    Help pickup-ready customers complete their orders and improve conversion.

    Hypotheses

    Translating goals into design hypotheses

    1. Stronger CTA visibility would help users find the pickup action faster.

    2. Clearer store states and actions would reduce confusion and mistaken store-card clicks.

    3. A clearer path to pickup would convert more existing purchase intent into pickup orders.

    Store card logic

    Show the right action for
    each product and store state

    I defined card states based on pickup eligibility, product availability, purchase rules, and stock status so users could immediately understand what was available and what they could do next.

    Success Metrics

    Defining Business Impact

    1. Pickup conversion

    2. Total Pickup Orders

    3. Total Pickup Order Value

    Final Solution

    Result

    Finding new business value
    through UX

    These results suggest there was still room to improve the pickup journey and uncover additional opportunities through clearer, better-placed actions.

    Pickup Conversions

    326.2%

    Total Pickup Orders

    132.6%

    Total Pickup Order Value

    132.1%

    Reflection

    Clearer actions can uncover demand.

    This project showed me how small points of confusion can stand between purchase intent and an order. Following unexpected behavior in the data helped reveal an opportunity that a broader redesign had missed.
